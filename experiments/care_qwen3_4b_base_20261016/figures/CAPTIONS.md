# Figure captions

Original figures 1–7 show real Qwen3-4B-Base measurements on fictional conditional rules; the additional clinical figures are described below. These are not clinical results. Layer and head indices are zero-based. The shared rule grammar limits generalization to unseen rule families.

1. **O1_faithfulness_misuse.** (a) Applicable versus nonapplicable conditional-rule accuracy for the unmodified model, official SEKA and official PASTA. All three methods coincide on the 12 held-out paired groups (24 cases per method). (b) Paired changes in applicable accuracy, nonapplicable accuracy and selection of the inapplicable action. Error bars are 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 group-bootstrap draws, resampling each complete rule group with both patients. Zero-width change intervals describe the observed empirical sample and do not establish population equivalence. Clinically verified prior-conflict eligible n=0; therefore the clinical prior-conflict hypothesis is not testable.

2. **O2_pca_pairwise.** Mean action-span pre-RoPE Keys at layer 2, KV head 0, chosen using development-only relevance displacement consistency. A single PCA was fitted to all four states of the 36 training groups; both panels use those same axes and only plot the 12 test groups. RA/RN are relevant questions with applicable/nonapplicable patients; IA/IN are irrelevant questions. Arrows contrast question relevance (left) or applicability (right). Different question wording/length and numeric changes are potential confounds. Separation is not causal evidence.

3. **O2_layer_head_heatmaps.** All 36 layers and 8 KV heads, without test-based selection. Key displacement is divided by the corresponding mean training Key norm. Relevance and applicability linear probes are standardized and fitted on training groups only; the heatmaps report AUROC on the 12 held-out groups. GroupKFold validation, exact within-group label-flip tests and Benjamini–Hochberg corrections across 288 heads are saved separately. Numerically predictable applicability does not establish abstract rule understanding.

4. **O2_controls_and_probes.** Development-selected head: normalized relevance, applicability and same-side numeric displacements, and independently evaluated linear probes. Error bars use group-level bootstrap. Cross-threshold values differ by four units; same-side values differ by one, so raw magnitudes alone are not a semantic applicability test. Per-unit distance comparisons are included in layer_head_stats.csv.

5. **O3_z_histogram.** Distribution of fixed-query projected-Key alignment z at all last 10 layers, all 8 KV heads and all 32 mapped query heads. The primary official positive/negative operator uses (P_pos+0.25P_neg)/2, fitted on training groups. There are 23,040 token–query-head–layer records from 24 patients in 12 independent rule groups; one |z|≤1e-6 record is excluded from direction rates. The display is limited to the 99.9% absolute-z quantile; statistics include all retained records. Confidence intervals resample rule groups, not individual heads or tokens.

6. **O3_weight_delta_scatter.** Measured local BF16 pre-RoPE Key editing followed by the model's RoPE arithmetic, with the original post-RoPE query held fixed. Unit oracle weights and a nondegenerate designed magnitude c=distance/(distance+0.1*threshold), r=0.75 are shown separately; lambda=0.8. Orange points have z<0. BF16 rounding and the float32 algebra identity are reported separately. These are local attention logits, not end-to-end changes in model decisions.

7. **O3_mismatch_heatmap.** Per-layer, per-query-head mismatch frequency for the official positive/negative combination and the positive projector alone. All heads were retained. Each KV head supplies four query heads (query_head//4). The scalar formula was checked in float32; realized BF16 direction changes and attention probabilities are stored in logits.csv. Spectral negative subspaces do not represent negative clinical applicability.

# Clinical O1 follow-up

These three additional figures use authentic clinical reference rules with simulated patients, not altered counterfactual medical guidelines. Screening requires source verification, stable choice under two closed-book formulations and disagreement with rule gold. No confidence threshold is imposed. All three methods are evaluated out of their clinical-rule family's training/development fold.

- **O1_clinical_faithfulness_misuse:** 153 eligible cases (78 applicable, 75 nonapplicable) from 12 rule families. Left: applicable/nonapplicable accuracy with family-cluster 95% bootstrap intervals. Right: paired changes in accuracy and selection of the highlighted, inapplicable action. Intervals use 10,000 family resamples and fixed fitted projections.
- **O1_clinical_screening:** Screening funnel and candidate/eligible counts for every clinical family, including families with zero eligible cases. Only screening counts are plotted; these are not independent efficacy estimates.
- **O1_clinical_subset_sensitivity:** Harmonic accuracy with family-bootstrap intervals for all 384 candidate cases, eligible cases, and both-eligible complete pairs (25 pairs, 50 cases). Denominators and family counts are shown separately; these subsets are not pooled.

Source verification does not imply independent clinician adjudication. Stable closed-book error is an operational conflict definition and may include answer-option preference. Confidence intervals do not capture model-refitting uncertainty or justify clinical safety claims.
